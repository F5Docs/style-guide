# Parentheses

## Guidance

Use parentheses only for these purposes: to reference another part of the text, to identify a specific instance with a number or letter, to number work steps, to spell out an abbreviation, to give a brief aside or alternative, or to show a singular and plural form together. Don't use parentheses for anything else.

## Examples

**Do:**
> Configure the upstream block (see Load balancing methods).

> Remove the outdated directives (lines 12 and 18).

> A Transport Layer Security (TLS) certificate is required for HTTPS.

> Before you restart, back up the configuration file(s).

**Don't:**
> The module (which was added in NGINX Plus R30) supports active
> health checks.
> (Use a separate sentence instead of a parenthetical aside for a
> full clause like this.)

## Notes

Approved uses:
- Reference another part of the text: "Configure the upstream block (see Load balancing methods)."
- Identify a specific instance with a number or letter: "Remove the outdated directives (lines 12 and 18)."
- Number work steps: "(1) Edit the configuration file. (2) Reload NGINX."
- Spell out an abbreviation on first use: "a Transport Layer Security (TLS) certificate."
- Give the singular and plural form of a noun together: "restart the worker process(es)."
- Add a brief explanation of a word or part of a sentence: "Increase the connection limit gradually (no more than 100 per minute)."
- Give an alternative: "Edit the primary (backup) configuration file."

When you count words toward the sentence-length limit, text in parentheses counts as one word in its surrounding sentence -- see [Sentence length](../voice-and-tone/sentence-length.md).

Don't use parentheses to hold a full independent clause as an aside. If the aside is important enough to include, give it its own sentence.
